Case study

Cutting a flat-traffic bill that had grown 60%

Role: sole cloud engineer 2023–2024 Retail client · details generalised

Problem

The monthly bill had grown about 60% across two quarters while request volume stayed flat. Finance wanted a number they could forecast against; engineering wanted to stop being asked about it every month.

My role

Sole cloud engineer on the review. The platform team owned the application changes; I owned the infrastructure analysis, the CDK changes, and the cost model handed to finance.

Architecture

Key decisions

  • Spot for batch, on-demand for the API

    Rejected all-Spot: the API's interruption budget was too small to defend. Splitting by workload got most of the saving without a reliability argument we'd lose.

  • Right-size from observed p95, not from the instance catalogue

    Rejected the "one size down everywhere" pass: two services were genuinely memory-bound and would have started failing under load.

  • Budget alerts in CDK, not the console

    Rejected console-configured budgets: nothing reviewable in a PR is nothing that survives a team change.

Outcome

34%

Monthly spend

0

Reliability regressions

±6%

Forecast accuracy

Measured against the 2023 monthly average, over the six months after the change, at comparable request volume. Storage cost was unchanged — the growth there was real and the fix was a retention policy the client chose not to adopt.
