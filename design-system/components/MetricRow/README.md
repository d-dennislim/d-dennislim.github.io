# MetricRow

A row of two to four figures, each a `metric` number over a `label` caption. Lives in the hero's console panel, at the head of a case study, and nowhere else — a page that counts things in three places reads as padding.

The consumer provides: each figure and its label, and optionally a one-line note under a figure saying what it's measured against.

Do:
- Use figures you can defend in an interview, with the basis stated somewhere on the page ("34% — monthly EC2 spend, 2023 baseline vs. 2024"). An unqualified percentage is the single most common thing an interviewer probes.
- Keep labels to one or two words in `label`; the note carries any qualification.
- Prefer a real absolute alongside a percentage where the absolute is unremarkable — "34% ($4.2k/mo)" is more credible than either alone.

Don't:
- Don't put a metric here that isn't attributable to work you did, and don't round a number up to look better; if you can't source it, cut the tile.
- Don't exceed four tiles — the row stops scanning as a set.
- Don't use it for a count of technologies or years; those aren't outcomes.
